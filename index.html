Lorekeeper

A research-grounded content studio for educational creators. It learns your brand, researches your niche, and turns what it finds into original ideas, with every claim tied to a source you can check.

Your profile is saved in this browser only. Nothing is stored on a server. Lorekeeper does not connect to or scrape any social media account: you choose what to share by pasting it here.

Who you are as a creator

Research and generate ideas

Lorekeeper reads your profile, analyses your past posts, searches the web for studies, primary texts and stories in your niche, then proposes ideas. A full run usually takes 30–90 seconds.

Learn from another creator, without copying them

Describe a post you admire. Lorekeeper separates the transferable structure from what belongs to that creator, builds an original idea in your voice, and writes a credit line.

Decision log

Every idea you keep or reject is recorded here with your reason. The AI suggests; you decide. This log is the record of that judgement.

How Lorekeeper works

  1. Brand profile. You describe your niche, voice and pillars and paste your own post data. No account connection, no scraping.
  2. Research. Claude (Anthropic) runs live web searches for substantive material in your niche.
  3. Generation. Ideas are written to fit your profile and past performance, each with a hook, outline, accuracy risk and craft tip.
  4. Source check. The server compares every cited URL against the search results actually returned. Anything else is flagged.
  5. Your decision. You keep or reject each idea with a reason, building a log of your own judgement.

What the source badges mean

Source retrievedThe cited page was actually returned by the search in this run. It still needs reading: a real page can be misread or oversimplified.
Source not retrievedThe model cited a URL the search never returned. Treat it as a possible fabrication until you check it yourself.
UnsourcedNo source given. Verify before posting, or cut it.

Where your judgement stays essential

  • Reading the source before stating a study's finding on camera.
  • Deciding whether an idea is genuinely yours, and crediting the creators who inspired it.
  • Choosing depth over engagement when the two conflict.
